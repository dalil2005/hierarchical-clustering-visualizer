A distance is small when things are alike. A similarity is large when things are alike. You can convert: cosine distance = 1 - cosine similarity.

How agglomerative clustering works

  1. Start with every point as its own cluster.
  2. Find the two closest clusters, using a distance and a linkage rule.
  3. Merge them into one cluster and record the merge distance.
  4. Repeat until only one cluster is left. The record of merges is the dendrogram.
  5. To get k clusters, cut the dendrogram at a height (the dashed line). Use the "Clusters now" slider.

Linkage rules

  • Single: distance between the closest pair of points. Can chain clusters together (try the bands).
  • Complete: distance between the farthest pair. Makes compact clusters.
  • Average: mean of all pairwise distances. A balance of the two.
  • Ward: merges the pair that increases total within-cluster variance the least. Always uses Euclidean distance here.

Experiments to try

  • Use Two elongated bands with Single, then Complete linkage and compare the cuts at 2 clusters.
  • Switch the distance to Cosine: points in the same direction from the bottom-left corner merge, whatever their distance from it.
  • Look for a big jump in height on the dendrogram. Cutting just below it is a good choice of k.
  • Compare with K-Means: hierarchical clustering needs no k in advance and gives a full tree, but costs more on big data.
